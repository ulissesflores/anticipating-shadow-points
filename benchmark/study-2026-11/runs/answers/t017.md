The repo is the Flask microblog tutorial app at chapter 23. It already has a background-job worker (RQ with Redis) and an email helper. It has no scheduler, no email preference setting, no unsubscribe flow, no record of sent emails and no email verification. There was no lessons file to consult. Web research came from known standards (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe, Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender rules), not from a separate research agent.

## Assumptions
- **A1** Everyone gets the digest by default and can opt out with an unsubscribe link or a checkbox on their profile.
- **A2** An external cron job (or Heroku Scheduler) runs a new `flask digest send` command on Mondays at 08:00 UTC. No new dependency.
- **A3** Each digest covers posts since that user's last digest, capped at 7 days. If there are no new posts, no email goes out but the "last digest" mark still moves forward. At most 20 posts are listed, then "+N more".
- **A4** Emails are in English only; the app doesn't store a language per user.
- **A5** Each digest is sent at most once: a failed send is lost for that week rather than risking a duplicate. Handling bounced emails and verifying addresses are out of scope.

**Validator:** the first round came back REVISE with 10 issues, all real and all now in revision 2. The main ones:
- Two jobs for the same user could both send.
- The time window could grow without limit.
- Mail providers' one-click unsubscribe would be rejected by a CSRF-protected form.
- Importing `tasks.py` in tests builds a second app against the production database and Redis.
- Mixing timezone-aware and timezone-naive datetimes raises `TypeError`.
- The `SERVER_NAME` check was in the command, but the worker is what builds the links.
- The default `SECRET_KEY` lets anyone forge unsubscribe tokens.
- Error logs would email the admins once per failed user.

`--standard` allows one validator round, so revision 2 has not been re-validated.

## Main risks
| # | Risk | Why it bites here | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Duplicate emails | Cron can fire twice and RQ can retry a job; there is no record of sent mail today | Cutoff rounded to the hour, fixed job ID, mark the user as sent before sending |
| 2 | Worker crashes rendering the email | `get_locale()` reads the web request (`app/__init__.py:17`), which the worker doesn't have | Force the English locale when rendering |
| 3 | Broken or missing links | Building full URLs outside a request needs `SERVER_NAME` | Check it in both the command and the worker |
| 4 | Own posts appear in the digest | `following_posts()` includes the user's own posts | New query that excludes them, with a test |
| 5 | Timezone `TypeError` or posts counted twice | SQLite returns timestamps without a timezone | Store and compare in UTC without timezone; window excludes its start and includes its end |
| 6 | One-click unsubscribe fails | The provider's request carries no CSRF token | Token-only POST handler plus a test |
| 7 | Forged unsubscribes | `SECRET_KEY` defaults to a known value (`config.py:9`) | Command refuses the default key; token is tied to its purpose |
| 8 | Spam folder or blocked sender | Unverified emails, sender is a placeholder (`ADMINS[0]`), no bounce handling | Unsubscribe headers, a `DIGEST_SENDER` setting, rate limits |
| 9 | Tests touch the production database or Redis | `tasks.py` builds the app when imported (`app/tasks.py:11`) | Logic lives in `app/digest.py` |
| 10 | Admins flooded with error emails | Each error log is emailed to the admins | Log per-user failures as warnings |

Riskiest: #1, duplicate emails sent to every user when cron or RQ runs a job twice. Users notice it at once and it hurts the sender's reputation.
