I read the repo (read-only, nothing edited). It's a Laravel RealWorld API. Comments have `id`, `user_id`, `article_id`, `body` and timestamps. `CommentController` has index, store and destroy. Users have no role or admin concept, there are no policies, and the only notifications are the default `Notifiable` trait. `CommentController@index` returns every comment to everyone.

## Assumptions

- "Moderator" means a new role on `users`, such as a `role` column or an `is_moderator` flag. Nothing like it exists today.
- "Hide" is a soft state, not deletion. The comment is kept, but the public API stops showing its body.
- The author can still see their own hidden comment and the reason it was hidden.
- One appeal per hide. It is allowed within 7 days of `hidden_at`. A moderator then upholds the hide or restores the comment.
- The 7 days are counted from the moment of hiding, and a restored comment stays restored.
- Notifying the author is out of scope for v1. The author sees the status through the API.

## What could go wrong (before planning)

1. **Privilege escalation.** If the role is mass-assignable, `UpdateUser` or `RegisterUser` could let anyone make themselves a moderator.
2. **Leaked hidden content.** Hidden bodies could still appear through `index`, the `$with` eager load, article payloads, caches or transformers.
3. **Moderator abuse and no audit trail.** Hiding is silent, so there is no record of who hid what or why, and moderators could hide comments from critics.
4. **Moderators moderating themselves.** A moderator could hide comments aimed at them, or rule on an appeal against their own hide.
5. **Appeal deadline errors.** Timezone or server clock problems, a deadline checked only in the UI, and an off-by-one at the 7-day boundary.
6. **Appeal abuse.** Spam appeals, repeated appeals, appealing someone else's comment, or appealing a comment that was never hidden.
7. **Race conditions.** A hide and a restore, or two appeals, arriving at the same time could leave the comment in an inconsistent state.
8. **Existing clients break.** The RealWorld spec has no `hidden` field, and `DeleteComment` and the transformers assume a fixed shape.
9. **Migration risk.** Adding columns to `comments` and `users` on a live table. Old rows need safe defaults.
10. **Cascade and deletion interplay.** If the author deletes a hidden comment, the appeal record and audit trail could be lost.
11. **Authorization gaps.** Auth is route-level middleware only (`auth.api`), so a missing policy check on a new endpoint exposes it.
12. **Untested legal and privacy needs.** Hidden content may need retention for evidence, or may need removing under privacy rules.

## Plan sketch (if approved later)

- **Schema:** add `users.role`, which is not fillable. Add `comments.hidden_at`, `hidden_by`, `hide_reason` and `appeal_status`, `appeal_body`, `appealed_at` and `resolved_by`. Optionally add a `comment_moderation_events` audit table.
- **Authorization:** add a `CommentPolicy` with `hide`, `appeal` and `resolveAppeal`. Register it in `AuthServiceProvider`.
- **Endpoints:**
  - `POST /articles/{article}/comments/{comment}/hide`
  - `POST .../appeal`
  - `POST .../appeal/resolve`
- **Output:** update `CommentTransformer` so non-authors and non-moderators get a placeholder body.
- **Tests:** feature tests for the role checks, the day-7 boundary (using a frozen clock), and the hidden-body leak.

## Main risks

| # | Risk | Why it bites here | Mitigation |
|---|------|-------------------|------------|
| 1 | Role escalation | `User::$fillable` plus `UpdateUser` are open to input | Keep role out of `$fillable`; test it |
| 2 | Hidden body leaks | `index` returns all comments; `$with` and the transformer are unfiltered | Redact in `CommentTransformer`; leak test |
| 3 | No audit trail | No moderation history exists today | Add an events table: who, when, why |
| 4 | Self-moderation conflicts | One role pool, no separation of duties | Block self-hide; a different moderator rules on appeals |
| 5 | Wrong 7-day window | Timestamps and the boundary are untested | Server-side check from `hidden_at`; frozen-clock tests |
| 6 | Appeal abuse | No rate limits or per-comment limits | One appeal per hide; author-only; throttle |
| 7 | Concurrent state races | Plain Eloquent updates, no locking | Transactions, row locks, state-machine checks |
| 8 | Client breakage | The spec has a fixed comment shape | Add fields only; keep existing keys |
| 9 | Unsafe migration | Existing rows, cascading FKs | Nullable columns; reversible migration |
| 10 | Policy gaps | Only route middleware exists; no policies yet | Policy per action; unauthorized-access tests |

Riskiest: #2, hidden comment bodies leaking through unredacted paths (index, eager loads, transformers), because that defeats the whole point of hiding.
